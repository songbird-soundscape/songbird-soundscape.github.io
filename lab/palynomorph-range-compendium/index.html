Palynomorph Range Compendium

Each column is one spore or pollen taxon, running from its first appearance datum (FAD, bottom) to its last appearance datum (LAD, top). Time runs downward like a core. Every column is a bundle of six hairlines, one per continent, in a fixed order. A bundle that is all colours means a cosmopolitan taxon; a bundle with gaps shows a provincial one.

These ranges are a working draft compiled from general knowledge, not a database. Most are form genera, whose ranges are global composites. Dashed lines mark low confidence. Verify against primary range charts before citing; the CSV at the bottom is there for that.

Reading the bundles

The hairlines always sit in the same order: North America, South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, then Australia and Antarctica together. Where a taxon's range differs between continents, the hairlines end at different heights. Lycospora is the clearest case here: it vanishes from Euramerican coals near 306 Ma but carries on in the Permian peats of Cathaysia.

Continents are present-day landmasses, so India sits with Asia even though its Permian floras are Gondwanan, and Greenland sits with North America. The pink horizons mark the turnovers from the descent page. Read across one and you can see which ranges stop at it and which start just above.
