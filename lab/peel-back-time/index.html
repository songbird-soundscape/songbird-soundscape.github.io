What the map shows

For each place, the rock that would be at the surface once everything younger than the chosen age is removed. Where today's surface rock is already older, it stays. Where it is younger, the map looks underneath: compiled stratigraphic columns (from wells, cores and outcrop sections) supply the buried layer where they exist; elsewhere the spot is hatched, because nobody has a public, dated record of what lies there.

Why the oceans vanish

Ocean crust is made at spreading ridges and destroyed at subduction zones, so almost all of it is younger than 200 million years. Drag past the Jurassic and the Atlantic closes; by the Permian only scraps such as the eastern Mediterranean remain. The map does not move the continents back; it simply shows which ocean floor existed yet.

Where knowledge is thin

Subsurface columns are dense in North America and New Zealand, patchy in South America and the Caribbean, and nearly absent from Europe, Africa, Asia and Australia in this open database. Ocean drill cores sample sediments only, typically the last 100–150 million years.
