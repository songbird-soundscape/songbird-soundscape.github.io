grains enlarged · click a bed

Each bed is filled with grains drawn from its own size distribution and arranged the way its transport regime would leave them: sorted by swash, stacked in cross-sets by migrating dunes, draped by slack-water mud, graded by waning storms, churned by burrows, or made in place by carbonate organisms. Grains are enlarged; their sizes follow the φ scale, and clay and most silt show as matrix tone.

Rules and where they come from

The physics is textbook first-order: one grain size, steady uniform flow, linear waves. Facies widths are schematic rules scaled by the controls, meant for intuition and for setting up the landscape model, not as a reconstruction of any one coast.

Describe a bed and the page scores it against standard settings, lists the evidence for and against, and estimates water depth. Add interpreted beds to a measured section to get a relative water-depth curve, then send the trajectory and cycle amplitude to the coast model. The scores are explicit weighted rules, listed in full at the bottom of this tab.

How carbonates work

Born, not deliveredMost carbonate grains are made where they settle, by organisms or by precipitation from warm seawater supersaturated in calcium carbonate. Sand and mud in a siliciclastic rock were eroded from land and carried in.
The carbonate factoryProduction is fastest in shallow, warm, clear, sunlit water and falls off with depth, muddy runoff and excess nutrients. A healthy factory can keep up with rising sea level, catch up later, or give up and drown (Kendall & Schlager 1981).
Texture records energyGrain size mostly reflects the organisms that made the grains, so sorting says less than it does in sandstone. Whether the lime mud survived is the energy signal: kept in calm water, winnowed where waves work (Dunham 1962).
Ooids need two thingsOoids grow concentric coats while rolling in warm, agitated, supersaturated water, as on Bahamian tidal bars. They mark high energy and high saturation together.
Early cementCarbonates harden quickly on the sea floor and at shallow depth, making hardgrounds and locking in open fabrics. Coal balls are the same early cementation acting in peat.
The Pennsylvanian factoryPhylloid algae built mounds; crinoids, brachiopods, bryozoans and fusulinids made the skeletal sand; coral reefs were minor. It was an aragonite-sea interval, which favoured aragonite-secreting algae (Stanley & Hardie 1998).
