How this is built: each Macrostrat column gives, for every half-million-year slice, how much rock was deposited and what share was coal, limestone, sandstone and mudstone. Those values are interpolated between columns (inverse-distance weighting). A "landness" score (coal and sand count toward land, limestone and mud toward sea) is contoured to draw the shoreline, and the cyclothem slider moves the threshold. Hatched areas are more than about 150 km from any column, or are fed by a single coarse unit spanning several million years, so the map there is guesswork. Uplands, channel traces and vignettes are schematic and hand-placed from published descriptions; channel courses are approximate. Present-day state lines are shown for orientation only. Paleolatitude (dotted lines, and the paleo-N arrow) comes from a plane fitted to the paleocoordinates Macrostrat gives for each unit (its plate model; 60 columns, typical misfit about half a degree): the whole region lay roughly 0–10° south of the equator and drifted north by about a quarter of a degree per million years, and paleo-north pointed toward present-day north-northwest. Eleven columns in Texas and Louisiana (Fort Worth Basin, Bend Arch, Strawn Basin, Llano, Eastern Shelf, Midland and Kerr basins, Ouachita belt) were added to reach the Dalton coal.

Ocean currents are a cartoon of Heckel's quasi-estuarine model, not a simulation. The upper layer (pale streaks) carries warm, low-salinity water off the shelf toward the open ocean, assumed here to lie through the southwest gateway. The lower layer (indigo streaks over a faint dotted track) appears only when the sea is deep enough for a thermocline (toward highstand): cool, nutrient-rich, oxygen-poor water flows back onto the shelf beneath it and rises where the shelf shoals (expanding rings mark upwelling). That bottom water is what kept the sea floor anoxic while the black core shales accumulated. Pale streaks in active river channels show the downstream direction.

Heckel's cyclothems: the strip under the time slider follows Heckel (2008, Fig. 5), who grouped Midcontinent cyclothems of all scales into ~400-thousand-year units, each built around one major cyclothem, and calibrated them so the Desmoinesian runs 309.4–305.4 Ma and the Missourian 305.4–303.0 Ma. Spike height marks major, intermediate and minor cycles. Within a grouping the cycles are spaced evenly, so individual ages are schematic, though their order is Heckel's. With "Follow" on, the phase slider tracks a sea-level curve built from them: a quick rise to each highstand (the core black shale) and a slower fall to the next lowstand (coal). Studied coals and channels were re-placed on this calibration: Springfield (≈ Summit coal of Missouri; ISGS) under Upper Fort Scott (Little Osage Shale), Herrin under Lower Pawnee (Anna), Danville under Altamont (Lake Neosho; = Upper Freeport, Falcon-Lang et al. 2011), Dawson under Lost Branch, Secor under the Post-Secor cycle, and Lovilia (≈ Blackoak ≈ Pope Creek) in the latest Atokan; Baker, Bristol Hill, Calhoun, CP-19 and Urbandale positions remain approximate. The facies maps still come from Macrostrat's own age model, so they can be offset from the cyclothem timing by up to about a million years.

Literature: hover a coal marker with a dashed ring (or tap it) to open the studies of that coal and its basin, oldest to youngest clockwise from the top. Only geology is included (stratigraphy, palynology, coal-ball paleobotany, petrology, geochemistry). The list was compiled from OpenAlex and checked by hand; every DOI shown resolves through doi.org, and works without a DOI (state-survey bulletins, theses, abstracts) link to the repository record. Threads join two works when one cites the other according to OpenAlex, whose reference lists are incomplete for older and grey literature, so a missing thread does not mean a missing citation. Hovering a card highlights the coals and cyclothems it covers on the time axis. "Copy all citations" puts a DOI-referenced list on the clipboard.

Township & range: township polygons from the BLM National Cadastral Survey (CadNSDI, PLSS township layer), reduced to centroids. The readout reports the nearest township and its principal meridian; the section number is estimated from position within the township and is approximate. Texas and other non-PLSS areas have no reading.
